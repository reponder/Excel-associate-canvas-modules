Topic 1: Set Up Columns, Rows, and Cells

Inserting, deleting, and resizing the building blocks of your worksheet

As your worksheet grows you will often need to insert rows to add new data in the middle of a list, or delete rows that are no longer needed. There are three ways to do each.

Insert a row

  • Right-click the row header (the number on the left) and choose Insert. The new row appears above the one you right-clicked.
  • Select the row header, then press Ctrl++.
  • Select the row header, then on the Home tab choose Insert Cells → Insert Sheet Rows.

Delete a row

  • Right-click the row header and choose Delete.
  • Select the row header, then press Ctrl+-.
  • Select the row header, then on the Home tab choose Delete Cells → Delete Sheet Rows.
  • Right-click a cell, choose Delete, then select Entire Row.
✅
Select multiple rows first
The number of rows you select determines how many are inserted or deleted. Select multiple row headers before using any method above to act on them all at once.

Inserting and deleting columns works exactly like rows, but you interact with the column headers (the letters across the top) instead.

Insert a column

  • Right-click the column header (the letter at the top) and choose Insert. The new column appears to the left.
  • Select the column header, then press Ctrl++.
  • Select the column header, then on the Home tab choose Insert Cells → Insert Sheet Columns.

Delete a column

  • Right-click the column header and choose Delete.
  • Select the column header, then press Ctrl+-.
  • Select the column header, on the Home tab choose Delete Cells → Delete Sheet Columns.
  • Right-click a cell, choose Delete, then select Entire Column.
⚠
One type at a time
You cannot delete columns and rows simultaneously. You can only delete multiple columns at once, or multiple rows at once — not a mix of both.

To delete non-consecutive columns or rows: click the first header, hold Ctrl and click each additional header, then delete. For a consecutive range, click the first, hold Shift, click the last.

Data that is cut off or rows that are too tall make a worksheet hard to read. Adjusting column widths and row heights is one of the most common formatting tasks.

Method 1: Drag to resize

Select the rows or columns. Move your pointer to the border between any two selected headers. When the cursor becomes a double-headed arrow, drag to your desired size. A tooltip shows the exact measurement as you drag.

Method 2: AutoFit (double-click)

Select the rows or columns, then double-click the border between two selected headers. Excel automatically sizes to fit the widest or tallest content — called AutoFit. It is the fastest way to clean up a messy worksheet.

Method 3: Set an exact size

Right-click the selected headers and choose Row Height or Column Width. Type the exact value and click OK. Alternatively, use Home → Format to access the same options.

💡
Did you know?
The maximum row height in Excel is 409.5 points (456 pixels)! The default is just 15 points (20 pixels). A fun Excel trivia fact.

Sometimes you need to insert or delete just one cell rather than an entire row or column. Excel lets you do this by shifting the surrounding cells.

Insert a cell

Right-click the target cell → Insert → choose Shift cells right or Shift cells down.

Delete a cell

Right-click the cell → Delete → choose Shift cells left or Shift cells up.

⚠
Use with caution
Inserting or deleting individual cells shifts data in unexpected ways and can misalign other columns. In most cases it is safer to use Clear Contents or the Delete key to clear data without shifting, or insert/delete entire rows or columns.

Topic 2: Set Up Worksheets

Add, delete, rename, move, and copy worksheet tabs

Every new workbook starts with one worksheet named Sheet1. As your workbook grows you may need more sheets — or you may need to clean up by deleting sheets you no longer need.

Insert a worksheet

  • Click the + (New Sheet) button next to the sheet tabs at the bottom. The new sheet is added to the right of the active sheet.
  • Right-click any existing sheet tab and choose Insert. Choose the worksheet type and click OK. This inserts the sheet to the left of the active sheet.
  • On the Home tab, choose Insert Cells → Insert Sheet.

Delete a worksheet

  • Right-click the sheet tab and choose Delete.
  • On the Home tab, choose Delete Cells → Delete Sheet.
⚠
Deletion cannot be undone
Deleting a worksheet is permanent — Ctrl+Z will not undo it. Always double-check before deleting. If you do accidentally delete, close the workbook without saving and reopen it — but you will lose other unsaved changes.
🔔
Selecting multiple worksheets
Hold Shift to select a consecutive range of tabs, or hold Ctrl to select non-consecutive tabs. Then delete them all at once.

A workbook full of Sheet1, Sheet2, Sheet3 tabs is confusing — you have no idea what each one contains without opening it. Renaming sheets to something meaningful (like January, Sales Data, or 30-Day Month) is one of the simplest habits for keeping work organized.

Three ways to rename

  • Double-click the sheet tab. The name becomes editable. Type the new name and press Enter, or click anywhere off the tab to confirm.
  • Right-click the sheet tab and choose Rename. Type and press Enter.
  • On the Home tab, choose Format → Rename Sheet.
💡
Naming rules
These characters are not allowed: * / \ ? : [ ] — they are reserved for system use. Spaces are allowed in modern Excel versions.

As your workbook grows you may want to reorder sheet tabs so related data is grouped together.

Method 1: Drag and drop

Click the sheet tab, hold the mouse, drag it to the new position, and release. A small triangle indicator shows where the sheet will land.

Method 2: Move or Copy dialog

1
Right-click the sheet tab and choose Move or Copy.
2
In the Before sheet list, select where to move it, or choose (move to end).
3
Leave Create a copy unchecked, then click OK.
Figure 1: The Move or Copy dialog — choose where in the Before sheet list

Copying a worksheet lets you reuse a layout without rebuilding it. You can copy within the same workbook or directly into a different workbook.

Copy within the same workbook

Quick method: Hold Ctrl, click and drag the tab. A + icon on the tab indicates a copy will be made. Release to place the copy.

Dialog method: Right-click tab → Move or Copy → check Create a copy → choose position → OK.

Copy or move to a different workbook

Both workbooks must be open. Then:

1
Right-click the sheet tab and choose Move or Copy.
2
Click the To book dropdown and select the target workbook (or new book).
3
Check Create a copy if copying rather than moving.
4
Choose the position in Before sheet, then click OK.
